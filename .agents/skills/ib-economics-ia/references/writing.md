# IA: from article to commentary

Source: IB Diploma Programme *Economics guide*, first assessment 2024, printed pp. 12–14 (concepts) and 67–73 (requirements and criteria). The outline and checkpoints below are coaching tools.

## Screen and document the article

1. Check the student's proposed article for a concrete economic event, policy or change that supports a syllabus model and a judgment. Identify the unit and why the case belongs there. Compare with the other portfolio entries for unit, publication and concept reuse. Favour reporting with facts the student can analyse rather than an explainer that supplies the analysis already.
2. Use published news media such as a newspaper, journal or internet news article, excluding TV or radio broadcasts. Check the publication date against the commentary writing date (within the preceding year), and preserve the original article. Highlight the relevant sections of a lengthy article. Prefer the same language as the commentary; otherwise include the original and an accurate translation of the entire article.
3. Record the article title, source and online access date when applicable, publication date, writing date, word count, syllabus unit and key concept. Keep a portfolio tracker with one row per commentary.

## Choose a concept that changes the argument

The nine concepts are **scarcity, choice, efficiency, equity, economic well-being, sustainability, change, interdependence, and intervention**. Choose one that fits the facts and is unused in the portfolio. Ask a question the concept makes answerable: *Who bears the adjustment cost?* points to equity; *Will spillovers make the policy less effective across trading partners?* points to interdependence. Name the concept, connect it to a specific article fact, and show how it informs the model or final judgment. No unit has a prescribed concept.

## Build a plan the student can draft

| Move | What to plan |
| --- | --- |
| Frame the issue | Identify the event, the central economic problem and why the chosen concept helps explain it. Use article facts rather than retelling the story. |
| Model the mechanism | Choose a model and note its relevant assumptions. Specify axes, curves or other elements, initial and final positions, and any relevant gaps or welfare areas. Explain what changes, why it changes and what that means for this case. A single well-used diagram can be more useful than several undeveloped ones. |
| Apply and analyse | Connect each key article detail to a model prediction, explaining the steps in between. Distinguish facts from assumptions and conditional predictions. Use technical terms precisely as part of the explanation. |
| Evaluate and conclude | Identify the most consequential trade-off, then explain which stakeholders, assumptions, time horizons or constraints matter here and why. Reach a conditional judgment if the evidence warrants it. Link it back to the issue and key concept. |

Use the sequence flexibly. If the student needs a word budget, suggest roughly 80 for framing, 350 for model and application, 280 for evaluation and judgment, leaving about 90 for revision. Diagnose the missing reasoning rather than offering a paragraph to paste: for a per-unit tax, ask which side is less elastic, how that shapes the incidence, and whether the article gives evidence for that assumption.

## Check the count and revise

Moderators stop at 800 counted words for each commentary. Exclusions include acknowledgments, the contents page, diagrams, labels of at most five words, diagram headings of at most ten words, statistical data tables, equations/formulae/calculations, in-text citations and references in footnotes or endnotes. Definitions and quotations belong in the body and count. Footnotes and endnotes are for references only. Cut repeated definitions and article summary before cutting the explanation of the diagram, links to the article, or the reasoning behind the judgment.
